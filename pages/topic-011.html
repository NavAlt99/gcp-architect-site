Google Global Infrastructure & Network Tiers

The physical foundation of Google Cloud: Regions, availability zones, sub-sea fiber networks (B4/Jupiter), edge points of presence, and Premium vs Standard Network Service Tiers.

1 Real-World Explanation

The Situation: Google's Secret Architectural Weapon

Google Cloud is built upon the same private planetary network that powers Google Search, YouTube, and Maps. Google operates hundreds of thousands of kilometers of privately owned subsea and terrestrial fiber optic cables. When you send traffic to a Google Cloud Anycast IP, your packets enter Google's private network at the nearest edge Point of Presence (PoP) and travel across Google's high-speed private backbone, completely bypassing the congested public internet. [1] [2]

2 Technical Discussion & Architecture Diagrams

Layer 1 — Foundation: Regions and Zones

A Region is an independent geographic area (e.g. us-west1 in The Dalles, Oregon). Each region contains at least three distinct Zones (e.g. us-west1-a, b, c) isolated against physical power, flood, and fire events. [1] [2]

Layer 2 — Practitioner: Network Service Tiers (Premium vs Standard)

DimensionPremium Tier (Default)Standard Tier
Routing PathEnters Google private fiber at nearest edge PoP close to user (Cold Potato).Transits public internet ISPs until reaching destination datacenter region (Hot Potato).
Global Anycast IPSupported (Single IP routes to nearest region worldwide).Not supported (Regional IPs only).
SLA & PerformanceGuaranteed high throughput, low jitter, and 99.99% network SLA.Variable performance subject to third-party ISP congestion.

Layer 3 — Architect: Choosing a Primary Region

Select regions based on: 1) Proximity to users (latency), 2) Regulatory data residency compliance, 3) Service availability (not all GPUs or services exist in every region), 4) Cost (pricing varies by 20-30% between regions), and 5) Carbon footprint. [1] [2]

Layer 4 — Staff: Subsea Cable Redundancy & Jupiter Datacenter Fabric

Google datacenters employ the Jupiter Clos network fabric, delivering petabits-per-second bisection bandwidth across compute racks. Subsea cables (Curie, Dunant, Equiano, Grace Hopper) provide multi-terabit redundant cross-ocean transport. [1] [2]

gcloud compute addresses create batch-ip   --region=us-west1   --network-tier=STANDARD
