Topic 009 Roadmap ID: 1.3 Phase 1 — GCP Foundations Type: service Verified: 2026-09-24

Identity Basics: IAM, Principals, and Service Accounts

The zero-trust identity perimeter: Cloud Identity, principals, Google Groups, basic vs predefined vs custom roles, policy bindings, and service account key-less security.

On this page
1 Real-World Explanation 2 Technical Discussion 3 Cloud City Analogy 4 Hands-On Demo 5 Knowledge Check 6 Further Reading

1 Real-World Explanation

The Situation: The New Security Perimeter

In traditional datacenters, security focused on network firewalls and perimeter fences. In Google Cloud, Identity is the new perimeter. Every API call (whether from a human developer, an automated deployment pipeline, or a VM querying a database) must be authenticated and authorized by Cloud IAM (Identity and Access Management).

The Three Golden Rules of Cloud IAM
  • Assign Roles to Groups, Never Individuals: When an employee joins or leaves a team, adding them to a Google Group automatically provisions or revokes their permissions across all projects.
  • Ban Primitive Roles in Production: Never grant Owner, Editor, or Viewer in production. Always use narrow Predefined Roles (e.g. roles/storage.objectViewer) or Custom Roles.
  • Never Export Service Account Keys: Storing key.json files on developer machines is the #1 cause of cloud breaches. Use Workload Identity and Service Account Impersonation instead.

2 Technical Discussion & Architecture Diagrams

Layer 1 — Foundation: Principals & Roles

An IAM policy binding represents: Who (Principal) can do What (Role) on Which Resource (Project/Bucket/Instance)?

Layer 2 — Practitioner: Service Account Types

  • User-Managed Service Accounts: Created by developers for specific workloads (e.g. app-prod@brightloaf.iam.gserviceaccount.com).
  • Default Service Accounts: Auto-created by GCP with overly permissive Editor roles. Antipattern: Always disable default service accounts in production.
  • Google-Managed Service Agents: Internal Google robots running background tasks (e.g. service-PROJECT_NUM@compute-system.iam.gserviceaccount.com).

Layer 3 — Architect: IAM Conditions & Deny Policies

Use IAM Conditions to restrict access based on time of day, originating IP address, or resource name prefixes. Use IAM Deny Policies to enforce hard organizational restrictions that cannot be overridden by project-level admins.

Layer 4 — Staff: Workload Identity Federation

Connect multi-cloud workloads (GitHub Actions, AWS Lambda, on-premises Kubernetes) to GCP without service account keys using OpenID Connect (OIDC) tokens via Workload Identity Federation.

Scope and planes

The resource model for Identity Basics: IAM, Principals, and Service Accounts must name its organization, folder, project, global, regional, zonal, or resource scope. Control-plane intent and data-plane traffic are different paths.

Practitioner defaults and troubleshooting

Set explicit project, region, identity, quotas, logging, and network dependencies. Treat undocumented internals as observed behaviour, may change.

Architect trade-off table

ChoiceOperational burdenScaling/consistencyChoose when
Managed optionLower toil, less controlPlatform-definedMinimize undifferentiated operations.
Self-managed optionHigher toil, more controlTeam-definedSpecific runtime or portability constraints matter.

Choose the managed option when operational simplicity dominates; choose self-managed when control is required; avoid either while requirements remain ambiguous.

Hidden dependencies and day-2 operations

Review DNS, IAM, metadata access, quotas, Google APIs, upgrades, capacity changes, migration off the service, and deprecation risk. Keep ownership, escalation, and landing-zone guardrails explicit.

Design-review questions

  1. What business requirement selects this service?
  2. What is its real scope?
  3. Which control-plane calls must succeed?
  4. What is the data-plane failure signal?
  5. Which quota or IP range saturates first?
  6. What is the blast radius of a bad policy?
  7. How are cost and usage attributed?
  8. How is rollback or migration tested?

What good looks like

  • Explicit scope and ownership
  • Least-privilege identity
  • Observable health and cost
  • Tested recovery and rollback
  • Documented alternative and exit path

Diagram Architectural Scope & Taxonomy: Why These Three Diagrams Exist

Architecture diagrams in this curriculum are not decorative illustrations; they are precision engineering models designed to answer three distinct, non-overlapping architectural questions for every topic:

D1 — Scope & Boundary Map (Structural Lens)

Question Answered: Where do execution and security boundaries lie?
Maps components into their isolation perimeters (Client Edge vs Project VPC vs Managed Services). Establishes trust boundaries, network ingress choke-points, and data-plane vs control-plane separation.

D2 — Request & Control Flow (Runtime Ingress Lens)

Question Answered: How does production traffic traverse the architecture securely?
Traces an end-to-end operational journey—from ingress initiation and IAM perimeter validation across Andromeda SDN to backend state persistence and audit logging.

D3 — Failure Injection & Resilience (Chaos Lens)

Question Answered: How does the system degrade, alert, and auto-heal under catastrophic failure?
Models the exact mechanics of component saturation, network timeouts, and health check evictions under stress, revealing observability signals and automated failover pathways.

4 Hands-On Demo

Hands-On Demo: Service Account Impersonation (Key-Less Access)
# 1. Create a dedicated least-privilege service account
gcloud iam service-accounts create order-reader   --description="Reads orders from Cloud Storage"   --display-name="order-reader"

# 2. Grant read access to a specific bucket
gcloud storage buckets add-iam-policy-binding gs://brightloaf-orders   --member="serviceAccount:order-reader@brightloaf-prod.iam.gserviceaccount.com"   --role="roles/storage.objectViewer"

# 3. Allow developers in group to impersonate the service account without keys
gcloud iam service-accounts add-iam-policy-binding   order-reader@brightloaf-prod.iam.gserviceaccount.com   --member="group:devops@brightloaf.com"   --role="roles/iam.serviceAccountTokenCreator"
