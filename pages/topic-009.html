Identity Basics: IAM, Principals, and Service Accounts

The zero-trust identity perimeter: Cloud Identity, principals, Google Groups, basic vs predefined vs custom roles, policy bindings, and service account key-less security.

On this page

1 Real-World Explanation

The Situation: The New Security Perimeter

In traditional datacenters, security focused on network firewalls and perimeter fences. In Google Cloud, Identity is the new perimeter. Every API call (whether from a human developer, an automated deployment pipeline, or a VM querying a database) must be authenticated and authorized by Cloud IAM (Identity and Access Management). [1] [2]

The Three Golden Rules of Cloud IAM
  • Assign Roles to Groups, Never Individuals: When an employee joins or leaves a team, adding them to a Google Group automatically provisions or revokes their permissions across all projects. [1] [2]
  • Ban Primitive Roles in Production: Never grant Owner, Editor, or Viewer in production. Always use narrow Predefined Roles (e.g. roles/storage.objectViewer) or Custom Roles. [1] [2]
  • Never Export Service Account Keys: Storing key.json files on developer machines is the #1 cause of cloud breaches. Use Workload Identity and Service Account Impersonation instead. [1] [2]

2 Technical Discussion & Architecture Diagrams

Layer 1 — Foundation: Principals & Roles

An IAM policy binding represents: Who (Principal) can do What (Role) on Which Resource (Project/Bucket/Instance)? [1] [2]

Layer 2 — Practitioner: Service Account Types

  • User-Managed Service Accounts: Created by developers for specific workloads (e.g. app-prod@brightloaf.iam.gserviceaccount.com). [1] [2]
  • Default Service Accounts: Auto-created by GCP with overly permissive Editor roles. Antipattern: Always disable default service accounts in production. [1] [2]
  • Google-Managed Service Agents: Internal Google robots running background tasks (e.g. service-PROJECT_NUM@compute-system.iam.gserviceaccount.com). [1] [2]

Layer 3 — Architect: IAM Conditions & Deny Policies

Use IAM Conditions to restrict access based on time of day, originating IP address, or resource name prefixes. Use IAM Deny Policies to enforce hard organizational restrictions that cannot be overridden by project-level admins. [1] [2]

Layer 4 — Staff: Workload Identity Federation

Connect multi-cloud workloads (GitHub Actions, AWS Lambda, on-premises Kubernetes) to GCP without service account keys using OpenID Connect (OIDC) tokens via Workload Identity Federation. [1] [2]

Technical references: Google Cloud resource hierarchy · Google Cloud SDK documentation

4 Hands-On Demo

Hands-On Demo: Service Account Impersonation (Key-Less Access)
# 1. Create a dedicated least-privilege service account
gcloud iam service-accounts create order-reader   --description="Reads orders from Cloud Storage"   --display-name="order-reader"

# 2. Grant read access to a specific bucket
gcloud storage buckets add-iam-policy-binding gs://brightloaf-orders   --member="serviceAccount:order-reader@brightloaf-prod.iam.gserviceaccount.com"   --role="roles/storage.objectViewer"

# 3. Allow developers in group to impersonate the service account without keys
gcloud iam service-accounts add-iam-policy-binding   order-reader@brightloaf-prod.iam.gserviceaccount.com   --member="group:devops@brightloaf.com"   --role="roles/iam.serviceAccountTokenCreator"
