1 Real-World Explanation

The Situation: The Evolution of Cloud Operating Models

As organizations scale beyond a handful of cloud projects, the traditional centralized IT ticketing model (where developers file Jira tickets asking operations engineers to open a firewall port or provision a database) becomes the primary bottleneck to business agility. [1] [2]

Leading cloud organizations adopt Platform Engineering. The Platform Team treats developer infrastructure as a product, providing "Golden Paths"—self-service, secure, automated templates that empower stream-aligned product teams to deploy autonomously. [1] [2]

The Three Operating Models
  • Centralized Operations (Legacy): Ops team owns and manages all infrastructure; developers throw code over the wall. High ticket queues, slow velocity. [1] [2]
  • You Build It, You Run It (Decentralized): Every product team manages their own VPCs, IAM, and servers. Fast, but leads to security drift, wasted spend, and duplicated effort. [1]
  • Platform Engineering (Modern): A dedicated platform team builds automated self-service internal developer platforms (IDPs). Guardrails are built-in by default. [1] [2]

2 Technical Discussion & Architecture Diagrams

Layer 1 — Foundation: Eliminating Manual Toil with Runbook Automation

Toil is manual, repetitive, tactical work devoid of enduring value (e.g. manually restarting deadlocked pods, rotating passwords). Automate runbooks using Cloud Workflows, Cloud Scheduler, and Cloud Functions. [1] [2]

Layer 2 — Practitioner: Establishing the Cloud Center of Excellence (CCoE)

A cross-functional CCoE unites enterprise architects, security leads, finance (FinOps), and developers to establish architectural standards, review patterns, and govern landing zones. [1] [2]

Layer 3 — Architect: Conducting Well-Architected Reviews

Schedule formal quarterly architectural reviews for all tier-1 workloads. Evaluate designs against the Google Cloud Architecture Framework pillars, identifying single points of failure, cost leaks, and security risks. [1] [2]

Layer 4 — Staff: Enterprise Systems Thinking & Culture

Staff architects transform culture: moving from blame to psychological safety, fostering continuous learning, establishing transparent technical RFC processes, and aligning engineering roadmaps with business revenue outcomes. [1] [2]

4 Practice Exercise

Practice Exercise: Designing a Well-Architected Review Checklist

Create a 5-question audit checklist for a team preparing to launch an e-commerce checkout service to production, covering Reliability, Security, Cost, and Operations.

Well-Architected Review Checklist

  1. Reliability: Does the architecture eliminate single points of failure? Are compute workloads distributed across at least 3 zones behind a regional load balancer with autohealing?
  2. Security: Are backend instances deployed with zero public IPs? Are service account keys prohibited in favor of Workload Identity?
  3. Cost: Are resources labeled with cost-center and environment? Have idle disks been removed and CUD commitments evaluated?
  4. Operational Excellence: Is the entire infrastructure codified in Terraform? Are CI/CD pipelines configured with automated rollback?
  5. Observability: Are SLIs and SLOs defined for checkout availability and latency with multi-window burn rate alerts configured?
