1 Real-World Explanation

The Situation: The Unforgiving Math of Downtime

At Brightloaf Bakery, the mobile ordering platform experienced three outages in one month. The VP of Engineering asked: "We use Google Cloud, so why aren't we automatically 99.99% available?" [1] [2]

The answer lies in the mathematics of Composite SLAs. When services are chained in series (Service A depends on Service B, which depends on Service C), overall availability is the product of their individual SLAs: [1] [2]

Composite SLA (Series) = SLAA × SLAB × SLAC [1] [2]

If each service has an individual SLA of 99.9% (0.999), the composite availability is:
0.999 × 0.999 × 0.999 = 0.9970 (99.70%).
That represents over 2 hours and 11 minutes of allowed downtime per month! [1] [2]

The Availability Math Table
  • 99% (Two 9s): ~7.31 hours downtime per month (~3.65 days/year). [1] [2]
  • 99.9% (Three 9s): ~43.8 minutes downtime per month (~8.77 hours/year). [1] [2]
  • 99.95% (Three and a half 9s): ~21.9 minutes downtime per month (~4.38 hours/year). [1] [2]
  • 99.99% (Four 9s): ~4.38 minutes downtime per month (~52.6 minutes/year). [1] [2]
  • 99.999% (Five 9s - Spanner): ~26.3 seconds downtime per month (~5.26 minutes/year). [1] [2]

2 Technical Discussion & Architecture Diagrams

Layer 1 — Foundation: Series vs Parallel Availability Math

To improve availability beyond any single component, place components in parallel (redundancy):
SLA = 1 - ((1 - SLA_A) × (1 - SLA_B)). Two 99.9% components in parallel achieve 1 - (0.001 × 0.001) = 99.9999%. [1] [2]

Layer 2 — Practitioner: Failure Domains & Blast Radius

Isolate failure domains: VM failure < Zonal failure < Regional failure < Global control plane failure. Architect workloads so a single zone outage never drops more than 33% of capacity. [1] [2]

Layer 3 — Architect: Exponential Backoff, Jitter & Circuit Breakers

Simple retries create a "thundering herd" or "retry storm" that crushes recovering backends. Always add randomized jitter: sleep = min(cap, base × 2^attempt) + rand(0, base). Use Circuit Breakers (Open, Half-Open, Closed) to fail fast when error rates cross 50%. [1] [2]

Layer 4 — Staff: Bulkheads, Graceful Degradation & Load Shedding

When saturated, drop low-priority requests (search recommendations, analytics logging) with HTTP 429 to protect high-priority transactions (order checkout, payment processing). [1] [2]

4 Practice Exercise

Practice Exercise: Calculating Composite SLA

An application relies on three components in series: (1) Cloud Run (SLA: 99.95%), (2) Cloud SQL Regional HA (SLA: 99.95%), and (3) Memorystore Redis Standard (SLA: 99.9%).

Calculate: (a) The composite SLA for the entire path, and (b) The maximum allowed monthly downtime.

SLA Calculation Solution

  1. Composite SLA: 0.9995 × 0.9995 × 0.999 = 0.99800025 (99.80%).
  2. Allowed Monthly Downtime: 30 days × 24 hrs × 60 min = 43,200 total minutes.
    43,200 × (1 - 0.9980) = 86.4 minutes (~1 hour 26 minutes per month).
  3. Architectural Takeaway: Even with high-grade managed services, chaining three services drops your end-to-end SLA below 99.9%! To fix this, decouple the cache or make it an optional non-blocking lookup.
