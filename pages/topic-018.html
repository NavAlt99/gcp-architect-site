Hybrid and Multi-Cloud Connectivity: VPN & Interconnect

Bridging corporate datacenters to Google Cloud: Cloud HA VPN (99.99% SLA), Dedicated vs Partner Interconnect, Cross-Cloud Interconnect, Cloud Router, and BGP dynamic routing.

1 Real-World Explanation

The Situation: The Hybrid Enterprise Reality

Most enterprises do not migrate to the cloud in a single weekend. For years, cloud environments must securely interoperate with on-premises corporate datacenters, legacy mainframe systems, and partner clouds (AWS/Azure). Google Cloud provides two primary hybrid connectivity vehicles: Cloud VPN over the public internet, and Cloud Interconnect over private physical circuits. [1] [2]

2 Technical Discussion & Architecture Diagrams

Layer 1 — Foundation: Cloud HA VPN (High Availability)

Cloud HA VPN provides a 99.99% availability SLA. It consists of two interfaces, each with its own public IP address. To achieve 99.99% SLA, you must configure two tunnels connected to two separate on-premises customer gateway interfaces with dynamic BGP routing. [1] [2]

Layer 2 — Practitioner: Cloud Interconnect (Dedicated vs Partner)

  • Dedicated Interconnect: Direct physical 10 Gbps or 100 Gbps cross-connect between your datacenter router and Google's colocation facility. Best for massive data transfer (>10 Gbps) and low latency. [1] [2]
  • Partner Interconnect: Connects through a supported service provider (Equinix, Megaport). Best when physical presence in a Google colocation facility is unavailable or bandwidth requirements are under 10 Gbps. [1] [2]

Layer 3 — Architect: Cloud Router & BGP Dynamic Routing

Never use static routes for hybrid architectures. Cloud Router uses Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) to dynamically exchange routes between on-premises and Google Cloud VPCs. Use Multi-Exit Discriminator (MED) values to establish active/passive priority configurations. [1] [2]

Layer 4 — Staff: 99.99% Interconnect Topology

To qualify for Google's 99.99% Interconnect SLA, architects must provision four VLAN attachments across two separate metropolitan areas (metros) and two separate edge routers (EERs). [1] [2]

Technical references: Google Cloud documentation · Google Cloud Architecture Framework
