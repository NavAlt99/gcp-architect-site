Hybrid and Multi-Cloud Connectivity: VPN & Interconnect

Bridging corporate datacenters to Google Cloud: Cloud HA VPN (99.99% SLA), Dedicated vs Partner Interconnect, Cross-Cloud Interconnect, Cloud Router, and BGP dynamic routing.

On this page
1 Real-World Explanation 2 Technical Discussion 3 Cloud City Analogy 4 Hands-On Demo 5 Knowledge Check 6 Further Reading

1 Real-World Explanation

The Situation: The Hybrid Enterprise Reality

Most enterprises do not migrate to the cloud in a single weekend. For years, cloud environments must securely interoperate with on-premises corporate datacenters, legacy mainframe systems, and partner clouds (AWS/Azure). Google Cloud provides two primary hybrid connectivity vehicles: Cloud VPN over the public internet, and Cloud Interconnect over private physical circuits.

The Pain

Without a deliberate Hybrid and Multi-Cloud Connectivity: VPN & Interconnect design, Brightloaf can see slow pages, failed orders, surprise spend, audit gaps, or a deployment that requires a weekend of manual work.

The Idea in One Paragraph

Hybrid and Multi-Cloud Connectivity: VPN & Interconnect gives one business problem a clear boundary, an observable lifecycle, and an explicit recovery choice. The learner should be able to explain the idea before seeing configuration.

Real-World Examples

  • Retail: a seasonal order spike must not become a checkout outage.
  • Healthcare: a records workflow must preserve privacy and an audit trail.
  • Media or gaming: a bursty workload must scale without making every dependency global.

2 Technical Discussion & Architecture Diagrams

Layer 1 — Foundation: Cloud HA VPN (High Availability)

Cloud HA VPN provides a 99.99% availability SLA. It consists of two interfaces, each with its own public IP address. To achieve 99.99% SLA, you must configure two tunnels connected to two separate on-premises customer gateway interfaces with dynamic BGP routing.

Layer 2 — Practitioner: Cloud Interconnect (Dedicated vs Partner)

  • Dedicated Interconnect: Direct physical 10 Gbps or 100 Gbps cross-connect between your datacenter router and Google's colocation facility. Best for massive data transfer (>10 Gbps) and low latency.
  • Partner Interconnect: Connects through a supported service provider (Equinix, Megaport). Best when physical presence in a Google colocation facility is unavailable or bandwidth requirements are under 10 Gbps.

Layer 3 — Architect: Cloud Router & BGP Dynamic Routing

Never use static routes for hybrid architectures. Cloud Router uses Border Gateway Protocol (BGP) to dynamically exchange routes between on-premises and Google Cloud VPCs. Use Multi-Exit Discriminator (MED) values to establish active/passive priority configurations.

Layer 4 — Staff: 99.99% Interconnect Topology

To qualify for Google's 99.99% Interconnect SLA, architects must provision four VLAN attachments across two separate metropolitan areas (metros) and two separate edge routers (EERs).

Scope and planes

The resource model for Hybrid and Multi-Cloud Connectivity: VPN & Interconnect must name its organization, folder, project, global, regional, zonal, or resource scope. Control-plane intent and data-plane traffic are different paths.

Practitioner defaults and troubleshooting

Set explicit project, region, identity, quotas, logging, and network dependencies. Treat undocumented internals as observed behaviour, may change.

  1. What business requirement selects this service?

  4. What is the data-plane failure signal?

D1 — Scope & Boundary Map (Structural Lens)

Question Answered: Where do execution and security boundaries lie?
Maps components into their isolation perimeters (Client Edge vs Project VPC vs Managed Services). Establishes trust boundaries, network ingress choke-points, and data-plane vs control-plane separation.

D2 — Request & Control Flow (Runtime Ingress Lens)

Question Answered: How does production traffic traverse the architecture securely?
Traces an end-to-end operational journey—from ingress initiation and IAM perimeter validation across Andromeda SDN to backend state persistence and audit logging.

D3 — Failure Injection & Resilience (Chaos Lens)

Question Answered: How does the system degrade, alert, and auto-heal under catastrophic failure?
Models the exact mechanics of component saturation, network timeouts, and health check evictions under stress, revealing observability signals and automated failover pathways.
